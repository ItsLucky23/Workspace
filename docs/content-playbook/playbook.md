# Content best-practices playbook

Curated, platform-agnostic craft knowledge the content AI grounds on when it writes a script, plans a video,
or designs a thumbnail. Each `###` section is one retrievable chunk. This is DOMAIN expertise ("what works"),
distinct from the product-knowledge corpus ("what the product can do") and from styles ("how it renders").

## Hooks & openings

### The first 3 seconds decide retention
Most viewers leave in the first 3 seconds. Open on the single most surprising, useful, or high-stakes moment —
never a logo, a slow intro, or "hi guys, welcome back". State the payoff or the tension immediately: show the
result, ask the burning question, or drop the boldest claim. A cold open beats any branded intro.

### Proven hook patterns
- Curiosity gap: promise a payoff and withhold it ("The one setting that doubled my battery — most people never touch it").
- Stakes/loss: frame what the viewer loses by not knowing ("Stop making this mistake with your...").
- Contrarian: challenge a common belief ("Everything you were told about X is wrong").
- Result-first: show the finished/transformed state, then rewind to how.
- Numbered list: "3 things..." sets a clear, finishable expectation.
Pick ONE hook and commit; stacking hooks dilutes.

## Scripts & retention

### Structure for retention, not for completeness
Front-load value, keep one idea per beat, and add a small open loop before each transition ("but there's a
catch...") so each beat pulls the viewer into the next. Cut everything that doesn't advance the payoff. Short
sentences, concrete nouns, active voice. Read it aloud — if a line is boring to say, it's boring to watch.

### Pacing by length
Short-form (< 60s): one idea, one payoff, no throat-clearing — every second earns its place. Long-form:
signpost the structure early ("first X, then Y, then the big one"), and reset attention every ~30–40s with a
visual change, a question, or a mini-payoff. End on a specific call-to-action, not a generic "like & subscribe".

## Thumbnails & visual first-impression

### Thumbnail principles
A thumbnail competes in a grid at tiny size. Make it read in under a second: one clear focal point, big high-
contrast subject, minimal text (0–4 words), and an emotion or curiosity trigger. A human face with a strong,
readable expression outperforms almost everything. Avoid clutter, thin fonts, and low contrast. The thumbnail
and the title should NOT repeat each other — they should combine into one idea.

### Title + thumbnail work as a pair
The title carries the specifics; the thumbnail carries the emotion/curiosity. Together they promise something
worth the click, and the opening must PAY OFF that promise within seconds or retention collapses.

## Platform-specific rules

### YouTube
Watch time + click-through-rate drive the algorithm. Optimize the first 30 seconds hard, keep chapters for
long-form, and design the thumbnail/title as the highest-leverage asset. Vertical Shorts favor a fast loop and
an instant hook; horizontal long-form rewards depth + retention resets.

### TikTok / Reels / Shorts (vertical short-form)
Native, fast, loop-friendly. Hook in the first second, keep it under ~30s when you can, and aim for a satisfying
loop (the end flows back to the start → replays). Captions/on-screen text carry the message with sound off.
Trends and sounds boost reach, but the hook still decides everything.

### LinkedIn
Professional, value-first, no clickbait. Lead with a concrete insight or result, keep it skimmable (short
lines, whitespace), and end with a discussion prompt. Authority + specificity beat hype.

## Ads & marketing

### High-converting ad structure
Problem → agitation → solution → proof → single clear call-to-action. Lead with the viewer's pain, not your
product; show the product only as the resolution. One offer, one CTA — competing asks kill conversion. Social
proof (numbers, testimonials, before/after) does the heavy lifting between the promise and the ask.

### Ad hook + creative
The first frame IS the ad — treat it like a thumbnail: stop the scroll with pattern-interrupt, motion, or a
bold claim. Match the message to the audience's awareness stage: problem-aware audiences need the pain named;
solution-aware audiences need why YOU over the alternative. Keep one message per ad; test variations of the
hook, not the whole ad.
